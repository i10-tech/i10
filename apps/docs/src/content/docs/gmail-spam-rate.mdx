---
title: Gmail spam rate
description: How to see how often Gmail users mark your mail as spam, using Google Postmaster Tools.
---

Gmail does not report individual spam complaints to any sender. Other mailbox
providers send a complaint for each message a recipient marks as spam, and i10
shows those. Gmail does not, so **the complaint counts in i10 exclude Gmail**.

The one place Gmail reports spam complaints is Google Postmaster Tools. It shows
a daily spam rate for your sending domain: the share of mail delivered to Gmail
inboxes that recipients marked as spam. It does not say which message or which
recipient.

## See your Gmail spam rate

<Steps>
<Step>

Open [Postmaster Tools](https://gmail.com/postmaster) and sign in with a Google
account.

</Step>
<Step>

Add the domain you send from, the one in your `From` address. Add the domain
itself, not a single address.

</Step>
<Step>

Google gives you a `TXT` record. Publish it in your domain's DNS, then choose
**Verify** in Postmaster Tools. DNS changes can take a while to spread, so try
again later if verification fails at first.

</Step>
</Steps>

Once the domain shows as verified, the spam rate chart fills in as you send mail
to Gmail users.

## What you will and will not see

- **Personal Gmail only.** The data covers mail sent to `@gmail.com` and
  `@googlemail.com` addresses. Google Workspace recipients are not included.
- **Volume is required.** With too little daily mail to Gmail, the charts show
  "No data to display". This is not a sign that something is wrong.
- **Your domain must be authenticated.** Postmaster Tools reports on mail that
  passes DKIM for your domain, which is what [DNS setup](/dns) gives you.

## What 0.1% and 0.3% mean

Google's sender guidelines set two numbers for the spam rate in Postmaster
Tools:

| Spam rate          | What Google asks                                           |
| ------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Below **0.1%**     | The target. Keep it here.                                  |
| **0.3% or higher** | Avoid ever reaching it, especially for a sustained period. |

0.1% is one complaint for every 1,000 messages delivered to Gmail inboxes.
0.3% is three for every 1,000. If your rate rises, look at who you send to and
whether they asked for the mail, and make unsubscribing easy.

## The Feedback-ID header

Every message i10 sends carries a `Feedback-ID` header. It holds an identifier
for your workspace made of letters and digits. It is not your workspace name.
Postmaster Tools groups complaints by this identifier in its Feedback Loop
view, so in your own Postmaster Tools account the mail you send through i10
appears as one group. i10 cannot see that view: Google shows it only to whoever
verified the domain.

i10 sets this header itself. A `Feedback-ID` header you add to a message is
ignored.
